# SceneRally Player Guide

This guide is prepared by the SceneRally team for people trying an interactive fantasy video story for the first time.

Official experience: [SceneRally](https://interactivestory.io/)

![SceneRally shared battlefield and story choices](assets/scenerally-public-product.jpg)

## Start with the shared scene

SceneRally brings a fantasy story into your browser. Its Angels vs Abyss setting gives viewers a shared battlefield to watch and two actions to consider. The appeal is straightforward: you can see the scene before deciding which direction you want to support.

Begin by watching the current video and reading the available actions. A useful first question is, “Which of these outcomes would I like to see next?” You do not need to write a prompt or set up a video project to follow the experience. The story, action descriptions and participation controls are presented together.

Watching is free. If you choose to take part with Credits, read the current on-screen explanation before committing. Credits are optional, and the site offers one-time Credit purchases rather than a subscription. Check the site's current help and payment information for details.

## Make sense of the two actions

The action cards describe different possible developments in the same story. Read both before choosing. The two choices are part of a shared experience, so supporting one is a way to influence the direction alongside other participants.

For example, a fantasy battle might offer a direct advance or a defensive response. The interesting part is the choice between outcomes: what each would mean for the scene and why one appeals to you. Use the actual cards shown on the site as the source of truth for the current round.

This is also a useful way to watch with friends. Pause the conversation at the action cards and compare the scenes you each hope to see. You can discuss a choice even if you only want to watch.

## Follow what became part of the story

The War Chronicle records completed events in the shared timeline. After taking part, it gives you a place to follow the story's recorded developments.

Keep the distinction between a proposed action and a completed event clear. The action cards show what viewers can support now; the Chronicle shows events that entered the story. Reading them together makes it easier to understand where the scene stands.

Battle Record & Honors provides a separate view of your participation. It helps distinguish the story's shared progress from your own involvement. The two views serve different reading needs: one follows the narrative, while the other follows your record.

## A short first-visit checklist

1. Open the official site in your browser.
2. Watch the scene and read the current story context.
3. Compare both action cards.
4. If you want to participate, read how Credits work before using them.
5. Use the War Chronicle to follow completed developments.
6. Open Battle Record & Honors to review your participation.

The quickest way to understand the format is to start with the video and the two choices, then use the records to follow what happens. The combination gives the fantasy scene a clear sequence: watch, consider, support and follow the story.
